🧠 Controller Adaptation Rationale & Acoustic Physics
Closed-Loop Optimization
S1 · Clear Shallow 45 kHz

At 10m depth with minimal turbidity (2 NTU), high acoustic frequency (45 kHz) maximizes range resolution and spatial imaging while drawing only 8.1 J (55% power reduction).

S2 · Coastal 35 kHz

Moderate turbidity and 30m depth align with nominal 35 kHz transducer resonance, delivering 25.4 dB SNR with balanced power draw (12.4 J).

S3 · Muddy Estuary 22 kHz

High turbidity (45 NTU) causes severe Rayleigh particle scattering at 35 kHz. Lowering frequency to 22 kHz preserves acoustic penetration, boosting SNR by +9.5 dB.

S4 · Deep Ocean 18 kHz

At 200m depth with 4°C isothermal profiles, low frequency (18 kHz) drastically cuts volumetric seawater absorption, keeping received SNR at 24.2 dB across maximum distance.

S5 · Arctic Edge 25 kHz

Sub-zero (-1°C) conditions introduce an acoustic ducting channel. The controller selects 25 kHz chirp modulation to mitigate surface-ice reverberation multipath.

🔬 Experimental Protocol & Verification Standard

TRG-VAL-2026-B

Tests executed across hydrodynamic acoustic tank facility (Trials S1–S3) and open-sea trials off Goa littoral waters (Trial S4). Fixed-parameter baseline locked at 35 kHz nominal pulse width (2.5 ms, 18.2 J). Adaptive TARANG payload dynamically computes matched filter response, real-time attenuation coefficients, and optimal pulse compression ratio with closed-loop latency < 42 ms.
